Joining a Virtual Community: The Complete Guide

print Print Article

What is a virtual community?

Virtual communities have been defined as a group of people who come together through similar interests or goals, where communication via electronic media is the primary form of interaction.[1]

Why are virtual communities important?

psychology The Adolescent Experience

Teenagers are in the pursuit of autonomy and independence from their parents, and being shifted into an environment (such as during a pandemic) which is restricting can feel suffocating. Furthermore, despite possibly making adolescents closer to their families, there can still be a drastic cut off in the amount of time they can spend with their peers.[2]

groups

The adolescent period comes with a heightened sensitivity to social contexts,[3] which makes it essential for them to be exposed to social interactions. This is where virtual communities come in.

Some of the ways that virtual communities help out are:

  • group_off 1. Countering Isolation They provide critical social interaction and connection, preventing feelings of deep loneliness.[4]
  • mood_bad 2. Countering Depression Community support acts as a buffer against depressive symptoms by fostering a sense of belonging.[5]
  • safety_divider 3. Providing Safe Spaces They offer a space for discussions about topics usually considered 'not alright' to talk about. This space makes people feel accepted and seen, especially marginalized communities like LGBTQ+.[2]
  • menu_book 4. Sharing Resources They provide peer-sourced resources and material to help out with specific issues, for example, a guide on how to deal with ADHD.[5]

How do you get into virtual communities?

Taking the first step to join a new community can be daunting. Here are a few ways to find the right fit for you:

  • search 1. Search Keywords Search up key terms related to your interests on social media platforms and the internet overall. Look for tags that align with your hobbies or the specific support you are seeking.
  • record_voice_over 2. Ask Your Circle Ask your friends and family what virtual communities they are already a part of and recommend. A personal recommendation can often help you bypass toxic or unmoderated groups.
  • web 3. Visit Official Pages Visit various pages of organizations or creators that you like and check if they host a virtual community. On Instagram, Google, and Facebook pages, they sometimes link servers (like Discord or Slack) which you can join.
  • forum 4. Check Internet Forums Explore established internet forums like Reddit and Discord for highly specific subjects of interest. Look for subreddits with active, clear moderation teams to ensure a safe environment.

Need professional guidance?

While communities offer great peer support, they cannot provide clinical care. If you are in distress or experiencing a crisis, please reach out to trained professionals.
