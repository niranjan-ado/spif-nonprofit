Suicide Trends in India:
What the Latest Data Actually Tells Us

A clear look at India's three-year suicide data trajectory. We break down the latest 2024 NCRB numbers, explore the economic pressures affecting daily wage earners, and highlight where prevention efforts are making a difference.

Analyzed by Niranjan Ramamurthy June 03, 2026 • 8 min read

insights Current Numbers

  • The Baseline: In 2024, India recorded 1,70,746 lives lost to suicide. The national rate is 12.2 per 1,00,000 people, edging down from 12.4 in 2022.
  • Demographics: Men make up 73.5% of these cases. Young and middle-aged adults (18–45) account for about 65.6% of all cases.
  • Occupation: Daily wage earners are the largest category at 31.0% in 2024, up from 26.4% in 2022. This is a share of the national total, not a rate within that occupation.
  • Reading this carefully: Suicide is almost never caused by a single factor, and a three-year movement of 0.2 in the national rate is too small to attribute to any particular intervention. We publish these figures as a baseline for prevention work, not as a verdict on it.
A Note on the Data

The data we discuss here comes from official police records compiled by the National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB). Because of the lingering stigma around mental health and legal complexities, many deaths go unreported or are misclassified as accidents. This means the actual numbers are likely much higher. We treat this data as a baseline to understand trends, not the complete picture.

Setting the Baseline

The National Snapshot

To understand public health trends, we have to look at the official numbers over time. Made available to the public in May 2026, the Accidental Deaths and Suicides in India (ADSI) report for the calendar year 2024 gives us our most current view of the situation across the country.

In 2024, the dataset recorded a total of 1,70,746 lives lost to suicide. Looking at the data across the last three years, rather than at 2024 in isolation, reveals some important shifts and reasons to keep pushing prevention work forward.

One caution before the numbers. Suicide is almost never the result of a single cause. The categories the NCRB records — occupation, stated reason, demographic group — describe circumstances that were present, not explanations. Financial hardship, illness and relationship breakdown appear frequently in the data and matter as risk factors, but they sit alongside mental health conditions, access to support, and many other things this dataset does not capture.

Trend Analysis

A Three-Year Trajectory (2022-2024)

Instead of just looking at 2024 in isolation, tracking the data from 2022 to 2024 helps us spot real trends in the nation's mental health landscape.

A Slight Drop in the National Rate

The total number of lives lost peaked at 1,71,418 in 2023, then dipped slightly to 1,70,746 in 2024. But the most important number we look at is the population-adjusted rate. Over the last three years, this rate has steadily declined: it was 12.4 in 2022, 12.3 in 2023, and 12.2 in 2024.

A drop of 0.2 is small, and three data points are not a trend. A change of this size sits well within the year-to-year variation you would expect from reporting differences alone, so we would not read it as evidence that any particular intervention is working. What can be said is that the rate has edged down rather than up during a period when support systems such as the national Tele-MANAS helpline were expanding. Establishing whether that connection is real needs several more years of data and study designs this dataset cannot support.

The Widening Gender Gap

The data shows a clear and widening gender gap. In 2022, men accounted for 71.8% of cases. That number climbed to 72.8% in 2023 and hit 73.5% in 2024. The dataset records the gap but not its causes. Researchers commonly point to a combination of factors, including provider-role pressure and consistently lower rates of help-seeking among men, though none of that can be read directly from these numbers.

Shifts in Age Demographics

We also see a slight shift in the age groups most affected. The 18–30 age group hovered around 33.1% in 2024, remaining the most vulnerable. However, the 30–45 age group has steadily grown from 31.8% in 2022 to 32.5% in 2024. Both of these groups combined make up over 65% of all reported cases.

Economic Pressures

The Daily Wage Crisis

Daily wage earners form the largest occupational category in the dataset, and their share has grown. In 2022 they accounted for 26.4% of recorded cases; by 2024 that had risen to 31.0%.

An important caveat on how to read this. These are shares of the national total, not rates within each occupation. Daily wage earners also make up a very large part of India's workforce, so a large share of any national total is partly a reflection of how many people are in that category to begin with. Establishing whether the risk is genuinely higher would require deaths per 1,00,000 people within each occupation, which the ADSI tables do not provide. The rising share is worth attention, and it is consistent with what frontline organisations report about income insecurity in the unorganised sector — but on its own it does not prove a causal link.

Other occupational groups shifted differently over the same three-year period:

  • Housewives: Saw a proportional decline, dropping from 14.8% in 2022 to 13.0% in 2024.
  • Agricultural Sector: People working in farming and agricultural labor saw a slight decrease from 6.6% to 6.2%.
  • Students: Showed a concerning rise, climbing from 7.6% in 2022 to 8.5% of total cases in 2024.
Understanding the Why

Shifts in Recorded Causes

When looking at the recorded reasons, 'Family Problems' remains the most common factor, increasing from 31.7% in 2022 to 33.5% in 2024. 'Illness' is the second most common cause, dropping slightly to 17.9% in 2024.

We also noticed cases attributed to 'Drug Abuse and Alcoholic Addiction' creeping up steadily—from 6.8% in 2022, to 7.0% in 2023, and reaching 7.6% in 2024. This highlights exactly why mental health support needs to be closely tied to addiction recovery programs and community support groups.

Taking Action

Prevention is Possible

Data is most useful when it helps us build better safety nets. Financial hardship is one of several recognised risk factors, and reducing it is one part of a much wider prevention picture that also includes mental health care, community connection, and accessible crisis support. Improving awareness of the schemes people are already entitled to is a practical contribution we can make to that wider effort.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the national suicide rate in India in 2024? expand_more
According to the latest NCRB data, India recorded 1,70,746 deaths by suicide in 2024, resulting in a national rate of 12.2 per 1,00,000 population.
Is the suicide rate in India going up or down? expand_more
While the total number of lives lost remains high, the population-adjusted rate has seen a slight, steady decline from 12.4 in 2022, to 12.3 in 2023, down to 12.2 in 2024.
Which age groups and occupations are most at risk? expand_more
Young and middle-aged adults between 18 and 45 account for over 65% of all cases. In terms of occupation, daily wage earners form the largest affected group, accounting for 31.0% of reported cases in 2024.
